## Slide 1 · The Problem (0:00 to 1:10)

Our school fees went up recently and a final year student who couldn't afford it posted an appeal so he wouldn't drop out. People gave. But then more posts appeared and my friends and I started to wonder if they were all real, because the screenshots looked identical and very easy to edit.

It's the same with appeals for hospital bills and the like. Most of us scroll past these, not because we do not care, but because dishonest people have made skepticism the rational response.

It costs nothing to do it. Someone opens a burner account on Twitter, posts a borrowed story with an account number, collects whatever comes in, and deletes the account by evening. Everybody knows that happens. That is exactly why people scroll past a real one.

Even with standard NGOs, when you give you cannot tell exactly where your money went, and can only hope it was not misdirected.

So donors hold back and real needs go unmet. The missing piece is not compassion. It's a trusted middle layer.

## Slide 2 · The Solution (1:10 to 1:32)

Kairos is this trusted middle layer.

As we will see in the demo, an AI interviewer thoroughly verifies that a story is credible and then helps the organizer publish a coherent cause. The money cannot be moved to the personal account of a donor, as it is held in escrow and only verified counterparties can receive payments.

## Slide 3 · Who It Is For (1:32 to 1:50)

Nigerians already give. The aim of Kairos is to make an appeal for help from a stranger as transparent as possible, and to make fraud difficult. It is built especially for people with genuine need to solicit for and get help with minimal hesitation.

## Slide 4 · What We Have Built (1:50 to 2:04)

The whole accounting and public ledger system, and everything you see in the demo, is live and running on MongoDB and Next.js. For now though, we simulated the payment, identity verification and escrow system.

## Slide 5 · What Is Left (2:04 to 2:14)

All we need to get this app running is an integration of what we simulated for now: the licensed partners for payment, identity and escrow.
