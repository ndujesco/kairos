# Faith & Tech · Application deck

## Slide 1: KAIROS

Kairos is a working web prototype for transparent giving. Its accounting, budget rules and donor updates run in code. Real payments and external verification integrations remain future work.

## Slide 2: KAIROS

The team first encountered this doubt around school-fee appeals, followed by similar posts with nearly identical screenshots. Avoid claims that every appeal is fraudulent or that transparency by itself establishes legitimacy.

## Slide 3: KAIROS

The current prototype implements the accounting, payment constraints and donor notifications. It does not hold donations or initiate real bank payments. AI helps structure the intake; independent document verification is not implemented.

## Slide 4: KAIROS

This is a native, editable illustration, not a receipt for a real payment. Assume the donor covers upkeep separately, so the full ₦7,000 reaches the cause. When 70% of the contributed funds are spent, ₦4,900 is attributed to this donor. The prototype calculates attribution from net contributions after any upkeep.

## Slide 5: KAIROS

Bank transfer, Remita settlement and NIN lookup are simulated. CAC and bank account-name verification are unbuilt. General file uploads and automated document verification should not be claimed as implemented. The Claude integration has a scripted fallback when unavailable. This deck makes no claim of live donors or a completed pilot.

## Slide 6: KAIROS

The cost-recovery model is proposed, not proven unit economics. The prototype shows the upkeep amount before confirmation and allows the donor to cover it. The 2% rate and ₦5,000 cap are tentative. Grants, credits and sponsorship are intended sources for shared operating costs, not confirmed awards or partnerships.

## Slide 7: KAIROS

These are launch plans. A pilot handling real donations has not been run. School fees would use institutional payment references such as Remita; hospital payouts require provider checks and a licensed partner.

## Slide 8: KAIROS

No safeguard makes fraud impossible. Provider checks and licensed custody must be operational before real payments. The prototype stores the result of the simulated identity check rather than a NIN, but this is not a claim that the wider launch privacy and security work is complete.
